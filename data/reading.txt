## Reading preferences

- Prefer high-concept-density writing, careful reasoning, useful mental models and arguments that change how one thinks.
- Strong quantitative literacy from physics research; still learning finance-specific concepts. Do not recommend elementary arithmetic exposition merely because a book is about personal finance.
- Examples, stories and case studies are welcome **when they reveal something substantive**. The objection is to thin arguments repeated for many pages, not narrative itself.
- Appreciate *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People* for reframing assumptions; also enjoy financial thinking, decision-making and personal development.
- Avoid repetitive popular non-fiction and overlong explanations of obvious ideas. Be candid about whether a book is worth the reading time.

## Suggested reading approach

Read for conceptual gain rather than book count. For each completed book, optionally record: (1) one or two useful theses, (2) a claim worth challenging, (3) whether it affects a real decision. Do not build a cumbersome reading-log requirement.

Before making further recommendations, check the current list and avoid re-recommending books the user has already rejected or found too shallow. For finance, differentiate **understanding a financial principle** from **choosing an asset allocation**; investment decisions also depend on liquidity needs, emergency reserves, jurisdiction and future obligations.
